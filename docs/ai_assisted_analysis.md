## 1. QuinStreet (QNST): top-ranked eligible company, subject of the LBO model

### What the business does

QuinStreet runs **performance marketplaces** for the financial services and home services industries: it attracts consumers through its own websites, paid search, and third-party publishers, and sells their inquiries to clients. **Clients pay only per action** (per click, lead, call, application, or customer), not for impressions, and QuinStreet bears the cost of buying the media. [Q1]

This model has two consequences an investor should understand:
- **QuinStreet's margin is the spread** between what clients pay per action and what QuinStreet pays to acquire the traffic. That explains the thin EBITDA margin (4.7% on our strict definition): revenue is large, but most of it pays for media. **Analysis**
- **It is asset-light** (low CapEx), but its "assets" are traffic relationships it doesn't control, mainly search engines and publishers (see risks). **Analysis**

### Revenue mix and growth (fiscal year ended June 30, 2026)

| Vertical | FY2026 revenue | FY2025 revenue | Growth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Financial Services | $888.4M | $817.2M | ~9% |
| Home Services | $405.4M | $276.6M | ~47% |
| **Total** | **$1,293.7M** | **$1,093.7M** | **~18%** |

Source: [Q2]. Home Services growth was boosted by the HomeBuddy acquisition (cash paid ~$105M in FY2026, per the cash flow statement used in our manual adjustment). **How much of the 47% is organic is not disclosed in the sources reviewed.** [Q3]

### Client concentration

- In fiscal 2025, **two clients accounted for 23% and 12% of net revenue**, and the **top 20 clients accounted for 62%**. In fiscal 2024, one client accounted for 12%. [Q4]
- Most client contracts **can be cancelled with little or no notice**. [Q4]
- **Not yet checked:** the fiscal 2026 concentration figures (in the FY2026 10-K). The FY2025 figures may have changed.

**Why it matters:** losing the largest client could remove roughly a quarter of revenue, and contracts give little protection. This is a central diligence item. **Analysis**

### Key risks (from the company's own risk factors)

| Risk | What the company says | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Search engine dependence | Depends on internet search companies "to direct a significant portion of visitors"; algorithm changes have reduced placements in the past | [Q4] |
| Third-party media | Depends on third-party publishers and strategic partners for "a significant portion of our visitors"; they can restrict or withdraw inventory | [Q4] |
| Regulation | TCPA violations carry statutory damages of $500 per call or text (up to $1,500 in some cases); the FCC has adopted new consent and texting rules | [Q4] |
| Past vertical declines | Has "experienced declines in our financial services client vertical," mainly from lost media supply and search algorithm changes | [Q4] |
| Client budgets | Adverse macroeconomic conditions, and tariffs raising insurance and home services clients' costs, could reduce client marketing spend | [Q4] |

### Reported vs. adjusted EBITDA: the key valuation question

| | FY2026 |
|---|---|
| Our strict EBITDA (operating income + D&A) | $60.6M |
| Company's Adjusted EBITDA | **$112.5M** (8.7% margin) [Q2] |
| Main difference: stock-based compensation | $37.4M (cash flow statement) |
| Other company add-backs | Acquisition costs, contingent consideration changes, restructuring, litigation, and other items [Q2] |

**The same company looks very different on each basis** (using enterprise value of $885M at the 2026-10-23 share price): **Analysis**

| EBITDA basis | EV / EBITDA |
|---|---|
| Our strict FY2026 EBITDA ($60.6M) | 14.6x |
| Company's Adjusted FY2026 EBITDA ($112.5M) | 7.9x |
| Company's FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA guidance midpoint ($155M) | 5.7x |

### Management's outlook

- **FY2027 guidance: revenue of $1.45–1.55B (~16% growth at the midpoint) and Adjusted EBITDA of $150–160M** (10.3% margin at the midpoint). [Q2]
- Management attributes growth to the shift of marketing budgets to digital and performance marketing, and says AI applications are improving performance and productivity. [Q5]
- Part of FY2027 growth will come from owning HomeBuddy for a full year (it was acquired partway through FY2026), so not all of the 16% is organic. **Analysis**

### Implications for Ridgeline

1. **The LBO base case is far more conservative than management.** Our model assumes 8% growth and strict EBITDA of $69M in FY2027; management guides 16% growth and $150–160M of Adjusted EBITDA. Even after removing stock-based compensation (~$40M), management's plan implies strict EBITDA well above our base case. **Analysis**
2. **The investment debate is therefore about earnings quality, not just price:** Is stock-based compensation a real cost? (We say yes: a private owner must replace it with cash pay or management equity.) How much growth is organic? Can margins expand while QuinStreet depends on Google and publishers it doesn't control? **Analysis**
3. **Next step:** add a **"management case"** scenario to the LBO model to test whether the deal works on the company's own numbers. If it does, the recommendation hinges on believing management's plan. **Analysis**

## 2. Willdan Group (WLDN): runner-up

### What the business does

Willdan provides energy-efficiency, engineering, and consulting services to utilities, government agencies, and private industry, organized in two segments: **Energy** and **Engineering & Consulting**. [W1]

### Fiscal 2025 results (year ended January 2, 2026)

| Metric | FY2025 | Growth |
|---|---|---|
| Contract revenue | $681.6M | +20.5% |
| Net revenue (contract revenue less subcontractor and other direct costs) | $364.8M | +23.1% |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $79.5M | +40.2% |

Source: [W1]. Segment contract revenue: Energy $576.1M; Engineering & Consulting $105.5M. [W1] Management reports "double-digit full year organic growth across key metrics," without a specific figure. [W1]

### The margin denominator matters

A large part of Willdan's contract revenue is **passed through to subcontractors**. Measured against **net revenue**, Adjusted EBITDA is about **21.8%**; against contract revenue, it's about **11.7%**; and our strict EBITDA margin on contract revenue is **9.2%**. Our screen's margin test (EBITDA ÷ total revenue) is therefore **harsh on pass-through businesses like Willdan**. **Analysis**

### Outlook and drivers

- **FY2026 guidance: net revenue of $390–405M; Adjusted EBITDA of $85–90M**, assuming no future acquisitions. [W1]
- Management links demand to **electricity demand from AI and data centers** and growing grid complexity. [W1]
- Adjusted EBITDA adds back stock-based compensation, the same issue as QuinStreet. [W1]

### Implications for Ridgeline

At the 2026-10-23 price, Willdan's EV of $1,089M is about **13.7x** FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA and **12.4x** the FY2026 guidance midpoint, much higher than QuinStreet on the same basis. Willdan offers **higher-quality, mostly organic growth at a higher price**, consistent with our scoring results. **Analysis**
