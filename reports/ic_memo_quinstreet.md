---

## 1. Recommendation

We recommend that Ridgeline **not pursue an acquisition of QuinStreet at current terms**, and add it to our watch list.

QuinStreet ranked first among the three eligible companies in our screen, and on management's own growth plan it would be an attractive investment: our LBO model shows a **25.7% IRR** even after a 30% takeover premium. But two problems prevent us from acting:

1. **The deal is too large for the fund.** At a 30% premium, it requires about **$794M of equity**, versus our **$330M** maximum per deal. Within that limit, the most Ridgeline could pay is **$13.59 per share, 18% below the current $16.52 price**.
2. **The investment case depends on believing management's plan.** On our own, more conservative assumptions, the IRR is **11.7%**, well below our 20% target. The gap between the two cases rests on assumptions we cannot yet verify: how much growth is organic, whether margins can expand while QuinStreet relies on traffic sources it doesn't control, and whether stock-based compensation should be treated as a cost.

We would revisit the opportunity if the share price fell to about **$11** (so that a typical 20–30% takeover premium would keep the offer within our $13.59 limit), or if diligence and co-investment interest from our limited partners supported the management-informed case (section 7).

## 2. Company overview

QuinStreet operates **performance marketplaces** for the financial services and home services industries. It attracts consumers through its own websites, paid search, and third-party publishers, and sells their inquiries to clients, who pay **only per action** (per click, lead, call, application, or customer). QuinStreet bears the cost of buying the traffic, so its profit is the spread between what clients pay and what media costs.

| Fiscal year ended June 30, 2026 | |
|---|---|
| Revenue | $1,293.7 (up 18%) |
| Financial Services revenue | $888.4 (up ~9%) |
| Home Services revenue | $405.4 (up ~47%, boosted by the HomeBuddy acquisition) |
| EBITDA, our strict definition (operating income + D&A) | $60.6 (4.7% margin) |
| Adjusted EBITDA, company definition | $112.5 (8.7% margin) |
| Stock-based compensation | $37.4 |
| Cash / revolver debt | $128.3 / $70.0 (net cash $58.3) |
| Market capitalization / enterprise value (Oct 23, 2026) | $943 / $885 |

The business is **asset-light** (capital expenditures plus capitalized software were about 1.1% of revenue), and its largest relationships are concentrated: in fiscal 2025, **two clients accounted for 23% and 12% of revenue**, and the top 20 for 62%.

## 3. How we identified QuinStreet

Ridgeline targets U.S. B2B services companies with **$40–100M of EBITDA**, consistent with a $2B fund writing $200–330M equity checks. Our screen narrowed the universe in stages, with every exclusion documented:

| Stage | Companies | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Listed companies in 10 business-services SIC codes | 182 | SEC EDGAR |
| U.S. filers with usable 10-K data | 125 | 46 foreign private issuers and 11 others excluded |
| Pass size gate (EBITDA $40–100M, recent annual report) | 8 | Calculated from SEC XBRL data |
| Pass manual review | 6 | Mistras (announced acquisition) and RMR (financial services) excluded |
| Eligible (net leverage below 3.0x) | 3 | QuinStreet, Willdan, Liquidity Services |

Our metric calculations were validated against a hand-built model of Mistras Group (32 of 32 metrics matched). We then scored the six reviewed companies on growth, margin, cash conversion, capital intensity, leverage, and valuation:

| Company | Score (base weights) | Top pick in weight scenarios tested |
|---|---|---|
| **QuinStreet** | **66.4** | **4 of 5** |
| Willdan | 64.7 | 1 of 5 (quality-focused weights) |
| Liquidity Services | 63.3 | 0 of 5 |

QuinStreet's lead is narrow (1.7 points) and driven mainly by its **lower valuation**. Willdan is the higher-quality business but trades at a higher multiple.

## 4. Investment thesis: why QuinStreet could be attractive

1. **Strong recent growth with operating leverage.** Revenue grew 18% in fiscal 2026, and Adjusted EBITDA grew faster, with the adjusted margin up 130 basis points. Management guides fiscal 2027 revenue of **$1.45–1.55B** and Adjusted EBITDA of **$150–160M**.
2. **Asset-light, cash-generative model.** Low capital needs mean most operating profit can service and repay acquisition debt. The company has net cash today.
3. **Structural tailwind.** Management attributes growth to the continuing shift of marketing budgets toward digital and pay-for-performance channels, where clients pay only for results.
4. **Inexpensive on management's earnings measure.** At **7.9x** fiscal 2026 Adjusted EBITDA and **5.7x** the fiscal 2027 guidance midpoint, QuinStreet looks cheap. For context, H.I.G. Capital's announced acquisition of Mistras (another company from our screen) values it at about **12x** our strict EBITDA, or roughly **9x** on its 2026 adjusted guidance, which suggests sponsors do price deals on adjusted figures.

**Our view on point 4:** we treat stock-based compensation as a real cost, because a private owner must replace it with cash pay or management equity. On our strict definition, QuinStreet trades at **14.6x** EBITDA, which is not cheap.

## 5. Valuation and LBO returns

**Transaction assumptions (both cases):** 30% premium ($21.48 per share), purchase equity of $1,226, refinancing of the $70 revolver, 3% fees, and $98 of excess cash used. Senior debt of **$441** (4.5x EBITDA including the stock-based compensation add-back, as lenders typically allow; **7.3x** our strict EBITDA) at 8.5% interest, with all excess cash used to repay debt. Five-year hold, exit at today's market multiple of **14.6x** strict EBITDA.

| | Ridgeline base case | Management-informed case |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue growth per year | 8% | 12% |
| Strict EBITDA margin by FY2031 | 6% | 8% |
| FY2031 EBITDA | $114 | $182 |
| Debt at exit | $313 | $198 |
| Ridgeline equity required | **$794** | **$794** |
| MOIC | 1.74x | 3.14x |
| **IRR** | **11.7%** | **25.7%** |
| Maximum price for a 20% IRR | $17.43 (+6% vs market) | $25.03 (+52%) |
| Maximum price within the $330M equity limit | **$13.59 (−18%)** | **$13.59 (−18%)** |

The management-informed case reflects management's fiscal 2027 guidance (about 16% growth, including a full year of HomeBuddy), assumed to fade to a 12% average; its later years are our assumptions, not management forecasts.

**Where the base-case return comes from:**

| Source | Contribution |
|---|---|
| EBITDA growth | +$1,030 |
| Multiple change (bought at 19.3x, sold at 14.6x) | −$533 |
| Net debt paydown | +$128 |
| Fees | −$37 |
| **Total gain** | **+$589** |

Paying a takeover premium on a company already valued at 14.6x strict EBITDA, then exiting at the market multiple, gives back more than half the value the operating plan creates. **The return depends heavily on growth**, which is why the two cases diverge so sharply.

**Leverage risk:** in the first year after closing, interest of $37.5 consumes more than half of EBITDA, and **interest coverage is only 1.8x** in the base case. A shortfall of roughly 15% in EBITDA would leave the company unable to cover interest from operating cash flow.

## 6. Key risks and mitigants

| Risk | Why it matters | Mitigant / diligence response |
|---|---|---|
| **Client concentration** | Two clients were 23% and 12% of FY2025 revenue, and most contracts can be cancelled with little notice | Review FY2026 concentration, client tenure, and renewal history; model the loss of the largest client |
| **Dependence on search engines and publishers** | Much of QuinStreet's traffic comes from Google and third-party publishers it doesn't control; algorithm changes have hurt it before | Analyze traffic mix by source and the share from owned websites; test media-cost sensitivity |
| **Earnings quality** | Adjusted EBITDA ($112.5) is nearly double our strict figure ($60.6), mainly due to stock-based compensation | Quality-of-earnings review; estimate the cash cost of replacing stock pay under private ownership |
| **Acquired growth** | The 30.6% three-year revenue CAGR includes acquisitions; acquisition spending equaled about 8.5% of revenue over three years | Separate organic growth; review HomeBuddy's performance since acquisition |
| **Inflated cash flow** | FY2026 operating cash flow was boosted by a $45.4 increase in accounts payable, which is unlikely to repeat | Normalize working capital in the model; review payment terms with publishers |
| **Regulation** | Telemarketing rules (the TCPA) carry statutory damages of $500 per call or text; consent rules continue to evolve | Review compliance processes and litigation history |
| **Client marketing budgets** | Insurance and home services clients may cut marketing in a downturn or under cost pressure | Stress-test a revenue decline scenario |
| **High leverage at closing** | Debt of 7.3x strict EBITDA and 1.8x interest coverage leave little room for error | Consider lower leverage (which raises equity required), or a structure with a co-investor |

## 7. What would change our view

We would revisit QuinStreet if **any** of the following occurred:

1. **The share price falls to about $11.** Shareholders expect a premium, so the market price must be low enough that a 20–30% premium still keeps the offer at or below **$13.59**, the maximum within our $330M equity limit. At that offer price, even our base case would exceed the 20% target IRR (at $13.59, the base-case MOIC is about 4.2x).
2. **Diligence supports the management-informed case**, specifically: organic growth of at least 10%, no client above roughly 15% of revenue on a forward basis, and evidence that margins are expanding without rising media costs.
3. **Our limited partners commit to co-invest** roughly $460M alongside Ridgeline, **and** condition 2 is met. We would not pursue co-investment on the management case alone.

---

## 8. Next steps if the opportunity is revisited

1. **Quality of earnings:** reconcile Adjusted EBITDA to cash earnings; estimate the replacement cost of stock-based compensation.
2. **Client analysis:** concentration, contract terms, and retention for the top 20 clients.
3. **Traffic and media:** sources of visitors, dependence on Google, and media-cost trends.
4. **Organic growth:** separate HomeBuddy's contribution; assess its integration and performance.
5. **Working capital:** normalize payables and receivables to estimate sustainable cash conversion.
6. **Regulatory and legal:** TCPA compliance and any litigation exposure.
7. **Financing:** confirm debt capacity and pricing with lenders; test a lower-leverage structure.
8. **Comparison with Willdan:** Willdan offers higher-quality, more organic growth (Adjusted EBITDA margin of about 22% of net revenue) but at **13.7x** Adjusted EBITDA. If QuinStreet's earnings quality disappoints, Willdan is the stronger long-term candidate, provided its price becomes more attractive.
